Experiences already running

Something already runs, but nobody measures it.

You already have something live: an app, a projection, a VR piece, a screen in the visitor route, a stand that travels. It works, more or less. But nobody knows who it actually reaches, whether anyone comes back, or whether it delivers what the business case promised.

Our method does not change because you already started. We measure first, then we adjust. The same three questions, applied to what is running today.

Step one is the baseline audit. We measure what is running against the right, the returning and the referring visitor, so the next decision rests on a figure instead of an impression.

Step two is the channels and the audience. Most experiences under-deliver because the right people never hear about them, not because the content is weak. We rebuild the route to the audience.

Step three is the adjustment. Renew what carries the message, retire what does not, and keep measuring on the same three questions. A single measurement round is a valid piece of work on its own.

How we measure

Three questions we hold ourselves to.

Reach and engagement mean little on their own. We measure whether the right people came, whether they came back, and whether they brought someone.

R1

The right visitor

Not more visitors. The ones your message was meant for, defined before anything is built.

audience fit / cost per contact moment

R2

The returning visitor

A second visit is the honest test of whether the first one meant something.

return rate / occupancy per slot

R3

The referring visitor

When someone brings a colleague, a class or a family, the message travelled without you.

referral share / word of mouth

We also work with a conceptual measure: engagement time multiplied by reach, divided by cost, spread across events and years and compared with paid media. A way of thinking about media value, not a hard ROI promise.
